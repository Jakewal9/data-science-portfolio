# How Turnovers Impact Winning in the NFL

# Problem Defintion
The purpose of this analysis is to determine how winning the turnover battle affected game results during the 2025 NFL regular season. Turnovers, such as interceptions and lost fumbles, can have a major impact on a game because they end an offensive possession and give the opposing team an additional opportunity to score. A team wins the turnover battle when it committed fewer turnovers than its opponent. This project will compare the turnover margin of each game with the final result to determine how often teams that won the turnover battle also won the game. By examining every regular-season game rather than only looking at season totals, the analysis can show the relationship between turnovers and winning on a game by game basis. The goal is to determine whether winning the turnover battle was a strong indicator of winning an NFL game during the 2025 season.

# Data Description
The data used for this analysis comes from the 2025 NFL season and was accessed using the Python package nflreadpy, which provides access to NFL data from nflverse. The dataset includes regular season game information for all 32 NFL teams. For each game, I used data on the teams playing, the final score, game result, interceptions, and fumbles lost. These statistics were used to calculate the total number of turnovers committed by each team and determine which team won the turnover battle. I then compared the turnover margin with the final game result to identify whether the team that committed fewer turnovers won or lost the game. Using nflreadpy allowed the game and play data to be organized and analyzed in Python to examine the relationship between turnovers and winning during the 2025 NFL regular season.

# Data Cleaning and Preparation
Before analyzing the data, I cleaned and prepared it so that each observation represented one NFL game from the 2025 regular season. Using nflreadpy, I filtered the data to include only games from the 2025 season and removed preseason and postseason games. I organized the turnover data by team and game and calculated each team’s total turnovers using interceptions and lost fumbles. The home and away team statistics were then combined with the game results so that turnover totals could be compared between opponents. Games were then classified based on whether the team that won the turnover battle won or lost the game. Games where both teams had the same number of turnovers were excluded because neither team won the turnover battle.

# Interpretation
The visual analysis shows that turnovers have a relationship with winning, but it depends on how they were measured. The first chart shows that teams that won the turnover battle in a single game typically go on to win the game. The second visualization shows that a team’s total number of turnovers committed throughout the season had only a weak negative relationship with its overall win percentage.

# Limitations, Ethics, and Reflection
I expected turnovers to have a stronger relationship with team success in the NFL. The interpretation shows that team performance is more complicated than one stat can explain. Another limitation is that the analysis only uses data from the 2025 regular season. With only 32 teams in the season level visualization, there are relatively few observations available for examining the relationship between total turnovers and win percentage. Using several NFL seasons would provide a much larger dataset and could show whether the pattern found in 2025 is consistent over time. It also treats all turnovers equally even though some turnovers are more impactful than others, like an interception or fumble returned for a touchdown. This project taught me that a statistic can appear extremely important in one context but have a much weaker relationship in another. Winning the turnover battle appears important within individual games, but overall NFL success cannot be explained by turnovers alone.
